Background
Why This Analysis Matters

Nigeria bears one of the heaviest malaria burdens in the world. Understanding how geography, wealth, and intervention coverage interact with disease outcomes is critical for targeted public health policy.

Dataset

The 2024 Nigeria Demographic and Health Survey (NDHS) is a nationally representative cross-sectional survey conducted December 2023 through May 2024 under DHS Phase 8. Children's Recode file (NGKR8BFL) containing 27,783 child records was used as the primary data source, with malaria RDT biomarker results for 12,795 children under five years of age.

Analyst's Background

This analysis builds directly on the author's undergraduate thesis; a 12-week field study on mosquito species distribution and breeding site ecology in Kwara State, Nigeria. The present project extends that inquiry from local entomology to national-scale epidemiological data, applying data analytics methods to the disease outcomes linked to the vectors studied at the grassroots level.

Scope & Variables

Analysis covers malaria RDT test results (ML0), ITN/net usage (ML101), fever incidence (H22), wealth index (V190), residency type (V025), and geopolitical zone and state identifiers. Hemoglobin levels (HW53) were examined as an anemia proxy. Only children with valid RDT results were included in prevalence calculations.

Tools Used

Data cleaning and variable labelling was done in Excel,while transformation and modelling were performed in Power BI Power Query. DAX measures were written for positivity rate calculations, conditional formatting, and KPI cards. The final dashboard was built in Power BI Desktop with custom background design and published for portfolio presentation.

Key Findings
What the Data Reveals

Six findings emerge from this analysis spanning geography, wealth, intervention coverage, and healthcare-seeking behaviour among caregivers of under-five children.

National Burden
42.56%
Nearly half of all tested children under five years tested positive for malaria by RDT, a rate that signals endemic transmission across the country, not isolated outbreaks.
North–South Divide
54%
North West zone recorded the highest positivity rate at 54%, followed closely by North East at 52%. South West recorded the lowest at 24.78% — more than double the difference between the two extremes.
Hardest-Hit State
456cases
Kebbi State recorded the highest number of confirmed positive cases among all 37 states and FCT, followed by Katsina (353) and Zamfara (343), all in the North West zone.
Wealth Gradient
48.95%
Children in the Poorer wealth quintile had the highest positivity rate at 48.95%. The Richest quintile recorded 31.37%, a 17.6 percentage point gap that reflects clear socioeconomic inequity in malaria burden.
Rural–Urban Gap
5.73pp
Rural children had a positivity rate of 45% compared to 39% in urban areas, a 6 percentage point gap that likely reflects differential access to healthcare, ITNs, and vector control measures.
Net Usage Gap
55.4%
Over half of surveyed children slept without any mosquito net the previous night. Only 43.98% slept under an ITN, revealing a critical intervention coverage gap despite Nigeria's national malaria control programme.

How This Was Done
Methodology

This analysis followed a structured four-stage process from raw DHS data to published dashboard.

01 · DATA ACCESS

DHS Program Registration

Access to Nigeria DHS 2024 (Phase 8) microdata was obtained through the DHS Program portal. Children's Recode file (NGKR8BFL) was selected as the primary dataset containing child-level biomarker, household, and geographic variables.

02 · CLEANING

Power Query Transformation

Cleaning was done in Excel and numeric codes were replaced with descriptive labels across all key variables. Rows without RDT results were excluded from prevalence calculations. State and zone codes were mapped to names.

03 · ANALYSIS

DAX Measures & Aggregation

DAX measures were written for malaria positivity rate, RDT tested count, total positive cases, and conditional KPI formatting. Cross-tabulations were built across zone, state, wealth index, and residency type to surface equity-relevant patterns.

04 · VISUALISATION

Power BI Dashboard

An interactive Power BI dashboard was built featuring a Nigeria state-level choropleth map, donut chart, stacked bar charts, and KPI cards. Zone and state slicers enable dynamic filtering. Dashboard background was custom-designed with public health thematic elements.

Policy Implications
Recommendations

These findings point to four evidence-based areas where targeted intervention could meaningfully reduce malaria burden among Nigerian children under five.

1. Priority Targeting of North West & North East

With positivity rates exceeding 50% in both zones — and Kebbi, Katsina, and Zamfara as the three hardest-hit states — resources for bed net distribution, indoor residual spraying, and community health worker deployment should be concentrated in these areas first.

2. Close the ITN Access-to-Use Gap

55.4% of children sleeping without nets despite national distribution campaigns suggests a behavioural and access gap — not purely a supply problem. Community-level education and follow-up verification of net use should accompany future distribution rounds.

3. Socioeconomic-Targeted Interventions

The 17.6 percentage point gap between the Poorer and Richest quintiles confirms that malaria in Nigeria is partly a poverty disease. Conditional health support schemes and free treatment access for children in the two lowest wealth quintiles would reduce this inequity.

4. Strengthen Rural Healthcare Infrastructure

Rural children face 6 percentage point higher positivity rate than urban children. Expanding community-based RDT testing, artemisinin-based therapy availability, and rural health post staffing would directly address this disparity.
